AMASAMYA v5.4.0 - Interaction Sweep engine

Check your website for accessibility problems. Free.

AMASAMYA is a browser add-on and a website that check any web page for problems that stop blind and low-vision people from using it. Made by a blind engineer. No sign-up.

Install the Chrome add-on Open the web version

What you get

AMASAMYA comes in two parts, and you can use either or both. The browser add-on checks the page you are currently looking at, or a whole website in one go (up to 200 pages). It compares each check against your last one and tells you what got fixed, what broke, and what is new. You can also set it to run checks by itself, say every Monday morning, and post the results to your team on Slack or Microsoft Teams.

The web version at amasamya.akhileshmalani.com does the same page and site checks in your browser without any install, and also checks documents (PDFs, Word, Excel, PowerPoint) and gives you a checklist for mobile apps. It creates reports you can hand to your manager, your compliance team, or a regulator.

Both are free. The add-on's source code is public at github.com/AMASAMYA/AMASAMYA.

Why this one and not another

Made by someone who needs it

AMASAMYA is built by a blind engineer who uses it every day. The tool itself works with a screen reader from start to finish, which most accessibility tools on the market do not.

Catches problems other tools miss

Older tools only check the code sitting still on the page. AMASAMYA also watches what happens when someone tries to use the page with a keyboard, so it catches broken menus, dead-end pages, and buttons that no keyboard user can reach.

Optional AI helpers you control

If you want, AMASAMYA can use an AI service (Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google) to describe what a page looks like, so a blind tester can check visual details on their own. You bring your own key. Your key never leaves your computer.

No "one-line-of-code" fix pretenders

AMASAMYA tells you what is broken and how to fix it in your code. It does not overlay your website with a widget that promises to make it accessible in one line. Those widgets do not work; the accessibility community has been clear about that for years.

Who is this for?

  • You build or maintain a website and want to know if it works for everyone before your users tell you it does not.
  • You are legally required to meet accessibility standards, either because your customers ask for it or because your country's rules say so.
  • You are blind or low-vision yourself and want an accessibility tool you can actually use without a sighted colleague reading the results to you.
  • You work in compliance, procurement, or as an accessibility consultant, and need reports your team can hand to auditors and regulators.
  • You are a designer or product manager checking your team's work before it ships.
